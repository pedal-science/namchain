Method
------
namchaind with the null backend (paced to the wall clock, so the numbers are real-time; silent input,
which the 2026-10-02 two-amp work found indistinguishable from played audio), q64, 60 s per arm, one
arm at a time, `engine.status` read at the end. Percentiles are from the daemon's own block-time
histogram, so they carry its 1% bucket resolution; "over90" is blocks at or past 90% of the 1333.33 us
deadline and "over" is blocks past it. The live arms use the kernel loopback card (the SSL 2+ was not
attached to piv): a real full-duplex PCM, 20 s, period 64, 6 periods.

--------
c2  two heavy chains: an A2-Full + cab per channel, one chain each   (the case the feature is for)

c4  four light chains: an A2-Full each, no cab
s2  the Ambient Wash: two A2-Full amps IN SERIES on one chain, + cab + delay, + the vocal chain
s1  that same serial guitar chain alone

Reading it
----------
- Where there are chains to share, the load roughly halves: c2 11/15 -> 6/8, c4 22/25 -> 11/15. That is
  the feature, and it is what it was built for.
- A one-chain document gains nothing (c1 5/7 -> 5/7; s2 11/13 -> 11/14). The Ambient Wash's two amps
  are in series on one chain, so there is nothing to overlap. The differences in the worst block are
  single blocks in 45,000, not a signal.
- Under memory pressure the two-core render is the one that holds the deadline: same load, same rig,
  one core misses it 4 times a minute (worst 119.6%), two cores never do (worst 53.6%). Each chain
  keeps its working set in its own private L2 instead of sharing one. The magnitudes of a memory-load
  arm are not reproducible (docs/tuning.md: the same flags gave 0 and 562 xruns 3.5 h apart); the
  ordering is the result, not the numbers.
- Pure CPU load on the housekeeping cores costs nothing (6/8 -> 6/9), which is the isolation working.
- The live arms replicate all of it on a real device. The single driver xrun appears in every live arm
  including the single-threaded one, so it belongs to the loopback card, not to the pool.
- Every arm: mlockall granted, every participant SCHED_FIFO 80, and the cores each participant got are
  read back from the kernel (the `render_cores` bitmask) rather than taken from the configuration.

What is not established here
----------------------------
- No thermal measurement: these arms are 60 s. A multi-core hour at temperature is outstanding.
- No edit-storm soak under render_threads 2.
- No audio-IRQ-placement arm: the SSL 2+ was not attached to piv, so there was no audio interrupt to
  move off a render core.
- The `dd` interference arms produced no status and are missing from the interference table.

The real interface (SSL 2+ is card II, irq 139 for its xHCI controller)
----------------------------------------------------------------------
Pedal settings: 8 capture channels, 6 playback, period 64, 6 periods, output queue 4/2. 30 s each.

ssl-t1        two chains, 1 core                    13%   16%   39.2%   0 over   0 xruns  22547 blocks
ssl-t2-irq2   two chains, 2 cores, IRQ on core 2     7%    9%   21.6%   0 over   0 xruns  22603 blocks
ssl-t2-irq1   two chains, 2 cores, IRQ on core 1     7%    9%   19.4%   0 over   0 xruns  22602 blocks
ssl-serial-t2 the series rig, 2 cores               12%   15%   31.9%   0 over   0 xruns  22602 blocks

- The pooled render works on the pedal's own interface with 0 driver xruns (the loopback card showed
  1 in every arm including the single-threaded control, so that one was the card).
- The two-chain gain holds: 13/16 -> 7/9.
- The audio-IRQ question is answered: moving the SSL's interrupt off a render core is worth 21.6 ->
  19.4% worst, which is noise at this load with zero overruns either way. Leave it where it is. The
  caveat is the load -- the render was at ~7% of the deadline here.

The edit-storm soak (the recorded ljoss session, 10 minutes per arm)
--------------------------------------------------------------------
Replayed by namchain-session-replay against 127.0.0.1, so this is the render path, not WiFi.

t1  451737 blocks  p50 5%  p99 11%  worst 36.2%  0 over  0 xruns  0 guard  0 base-mismatch
t2  451733 blocks  p50 6%  p99 11%  worst 37.3%  0 over  0 xruns  0 guard  0 base-mismatch
Both: 166 parameter ramps applied, 0 stale, 5 transitions, 1358 requests answered and none refused;
rig.apply p50 17.0 ms / max 19.7 ms, rig.patch p50 0.19 ms; the replay kept up (send lag p50 1.0 ms,
0 unanswered).

The two arms match because that session's rig is the single serial chain -- the one rig parallelism
cannot help. What the soak establishes is the other half: transitions and edits under a parallel render
are clean, single-owner (0 local_base_mismatches) and no worse than under one thread.
