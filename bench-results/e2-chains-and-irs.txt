E2 items 2 and 4: catch-up for chains, and an IR kept as a record
=================================================================

2026-10-06. Tool: namchain 083de32, `namchain-warm-bench chain` and `... ir`, built for aarch64 in
the image build's container and run under colima on the Mac. The chain numbers that matter for a
budget (wall time per catch-up) are repeated on vip in the same session as the density runs, and
are quoted below. Input: pedal.project guitar-di.wav (48 kHz mono 24-bit, 56.66 s, peak
-1.32 dBFS, rms -23.64 dBFS, md5 f9ab7f8f7ece122917d9701c3360a32f).

Item 2: does depth d need d x 340 ms of replay per model?
---------------------------------------------------------

A chain of four different library models in series -- Ampeg GVT52 CH2 NEUTRAL G5, Boss SD-1 Drive-I,
NAM Ampeg VT-40 Vol 4 M160, Ampeg SVT MD 421 -- each stage's state cleared, then caught up by the
procedure the tier design implies: stage k is fed the tail of the true input (k = 1) or of the stage
before it (k > 1), starting k-1 replays before the switch, so stage k costs (d-k+1) x replay samples
and the whole chain costs d(d+1)/2 single-model replays. The reference is the same chain run from
one reset at the start of the file. 7 switch points 8 s apart, error measured over the 200 ms after
each switch and then over the rest of the interval.

"identical" is the -1000 sentinel: bit-identical to the chain that ran all along, not merely close.

Reading
- **170 ms per model is exact at every depth from 1 to 4.** 85 ms is enough for depth 1 and 2
  (-115 and -144 dBFS) but not for 3 and 4 (-50.1 and -57.8 dBFS, at and over the -60 dBFS bar this
  project uses). So the per-model replay does not have to grow with depth: one model's memory plus
  margin covers every stage, at any depth. That is 170 ms against A2's 132 ms receptive field
  (GetPrewarmSamples() = 6347 at 48 kHz), which is the margin E1 also needed.
- **The plan's "d x 340 ms" is therefore 2x pessimistic per stage**, though its shape is right: the
  input history the whole chain needs is d x replay (680 ms at depth 4), and the work is d(d+1)/2
  single-model replays -- visible above as the cost roughly doubling per depth step (3.3, 9.8, 19.8,
  32.1 ms at 170 ms) and as the "replays" column.
- **This also reconciles E1's 340 ms.** E1's rig was a model *and* a cab IR in series, and a series
  chain's memory is the *sum* of its stages', not the longest: 6347 samples for the model plus 8192
  for the cab is 14539 samples = 303 ms, which is why its 8192-sample default replay left -51 dBFS
  and 340 ms was exact. The bench's default replay was `max()` of the two and is now the caller's
  choice; the mode above takes it explicitly.

Item 4: an IR kept as a record of its input
-------------------------------------------

A warm rig does not have to run its IR, only record what goes into it. The check: run the model over
the whole input to make the record, run the cab over that record once as the reference, then at each
of 9 switch points reset the cab and fill it from the last 8192 samples of the record and carry on.
nam::Linear's state is exactly its last receptive_field inputs, so this should be exact.

  switch (s)   err 0-200ms   err settled   fill (ms)
    6.0            -150.5        -150.5        0.597
   12.0         identical     identical        0.489
   18.0            -150.5        -144.5        0.529
   24.0         identical     identical        0.489
   30.0            -144.5        -144.5        0.507
   36.0         identical     identical        0.488
   42.0            -144.5        -144.5        0.505
   48.0         identical     identical        0.489
   54.0            -156.5        -156.5        0.592

  worst: -138.5 dBFS in the first 200 ms, -138.5 dBFS settled, fill 0.551 ms per switch.

Reading: it works. The residual is float32 rounding in the partitioned FFT (-144 dBFS is a
few ulps at these levels), and where the arithmetic happens to land the same way the two are
bit-identical. Filling costs about half a millisecond per switch, which is 4% of the 50 ms the
switch has to fit in. It only applies to rigs with a separate IR -- 35 of the 52 library models
carry their own cab -- and it is an optimisation, not a requirement: it buys back the IR's share of
the warm cost (about half a rig, per E1) at the price of one namchain change (record the cab's
input, fill from it).

The same run on vip
-------------------

Same four models, same input, same commit, cross-built in the image build's container and run on
vip with namchaind stopped. The convergence errors are identical to the Mac's to the digit -- the
deterministic half of this is host-independent -- and the wall times are what a catch-up would cost
on the pedal's housekeeping core. Low priority, no isolation: the raw cost, not a best case.

The cost is exactly d(d+1)/2 times one model's replay of that length: 4.93, 9.83, 19.61, 39.20 ms
for one model at 85/170/340/680 ms, and 49.22 = 10 x 4.93 at depth 4. So at 170 ms per model a
catch-up costs 9.8 ms at depth 1, 29.4 ms at depth 2, 58.8 ms at depth 3 and 98.2 ms at depth 4 --
which is the number tier 2's "Full catches up in the background" has to fit inside, and it is
tens of ms only up to depth 2 or 3.

Item 4 on vip: same -138.5 dBFS worst error, and the fill costs 1.257 ms per switch rather than the
Mac's 0.551 ms. 1.26 ms is 2.5% of the 50 ms a switch has to fit in.
