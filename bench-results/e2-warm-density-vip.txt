E2 item 3: how many models stay warm on one core (vip, 2026-10-06)
=================================================================

Tool: namchain 083de32, `namchain-warm-bench contention`, cross-built in the image build's container
(-march=armv8-a -mtune=cortex-a76) and run on vip with namchaind stopped, under
`systemd-run --scope --slice=pedal-bench.slice` so the scope could use the isolated cores 2-3
(system.slice and user.slice are AllowedCPUs=0-1 on this image).

Live rig: NAM Ampeg VT-40 Vol 4 M160 + York Audio ORNG 412 V30 421v-CNT (8192 taps), on core 3 at
SCHED_FIFO 80. Candidates: library models with **no cab** (that is what the density question is
about; the plan asks for the render tail beside them). Core 2 at SCHED_FIFO 70. q64, so a quantum
is 1333 us. 30 s per run, 22500 blocks. Input: pedal.project guitar-di.wav.

Reading
- **Eight Full models stay warm on one core; ten do not.** The bar is E1's: the warm thread's p99
  under one quantum, i.e. it is tracking the live input rather than falling behind it. At 8 it is
  89.1%; at 10 it is 112.9%, and at 12 the thread is 2621 blocks (3.5 s) behind and never catches
  up. Warm work only has to keep up *on average* for a candidate that a catch-up replay will repair
  at the switch, but a tier-1 candidate that is merely "usually" current is a candidate that is
  sometimes the wrong sound, so the p99 bar is the right one for tier 1.
- **Thirty Lite models stay warm; forty do not** (97.4% against 141.2%). The ratio measured here is
  3.7x, not the 5.8x the tuning numbers suggested, and it is the same story as the Full curve: the
  per-model cost grows with N as the models crowd the cache -- 6.6% of a quantum for the first Full,
  11.1% each at 8; 1.8% for the first Lite, 3.2% each at 30.
- **The plan's estimates were optimistic**: "~10 Full or ~50 Lite per core" is measured at 8 and 30,
  25% and 40% lower.
- **It costs the audio little, but not nothing.** With 8 Full candidates the render core's p99 rises
  from 8.08% to 10.60% of the deadline and its max from 11.7% to 24.2%, with no overruns in any run.
  The Lite runs' render columns are not comparable: --slim applies to the live rig too, so in those
  runs the live rig was a Lite model plus its cab, which is why its p99 is 3.65% rather than 8%.
  E1's numbers, where both sides carried the cab, are the ones to compare with for the render tail.
- **A competing job on the warm core changes nothing measurable.** The spinner is at a lower
  priority than the candidates, so the candidates still get their turn; what is left is cache and
  memory-bandwidth contention, and that moved the warm p99 by at most 5 points (Full 8: 89.1 ->
  94.3). This is the "housekeeping core under load" case, and it passes.
- **Memory is not a constraint and is not quoted from here.** The bench's per-candidate RSS came out
  larger for a Lite than for a Full (which cannot be right for a 3-channel submodel) because it
  measured what had been allocated rather than what the warm thread had touched; that measurement
  has since been fixed to fault the buffers in first, but the runs above predate the fix. For the
  figures that matter, namchain's own working-set model gives 587.5 KiB for an A2-Full and 99.7 KiB
  for an A2-Lite per block (docs/daemon.md, "Model budget"), and eight Fulls are a few MB against
  vip's 1 GB.

What this does not show
- Whether a warm set of *mixed* Full and Lite changes the cliff: the sweeps were all one or the
  other. A tier-1 set of 8 Fulls plus a tier-2 set of Lites on the same core is the configuration the
  tiers actually imply, and it needs one run.
- The bank sizes are the user's rigs, not this table: a bank of 8 rigs x 3 models is 24 instances,
  which fits at tier 2 (Lite) and does not fit at tier 1 (Full) on one core.
