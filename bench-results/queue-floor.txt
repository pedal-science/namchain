The output-queue floor: what it fixes, and the limit cycle it makes when placed wrong
====================================================================================

2026-09-15, `7a4d3dc`, SSL 2+ Mk II (hw:2,0), 4 x A2-Full, q = 64 (1333.3 us), 48 kHz,
`SCHED_FIFO 80` + `--cpu 3`, non-RT kernel 7.0.0-1017-raspi. Evidence:
soak-15min-floor4.{txt,env,heartbeat} (900 s) and soak-1h-cap4-floor2.* (3600 s).

What was added
--------------
Two things, because §9.5 item 8 had ended on a missing instrument and a mechanism it
could not supply.

  1. The occupancy DISTRIBUTION. The harness now keeps a histogram of the per-block
     queue occupancy, one bin per frame, clamped into the ring's length, and reports
     p1/p50 in periods plus the count of blocks whose queue held less than one period.
     That last number is the exposure window, and it is the one `min` could never give:
     `playback_occupancy_min` is 0 in every run that underran once, so it describes the
     worst run rather than the typical one. 3 KB per run, exact percentiles, no sorting.

  2. The FLOOR CONTROLLER (`--output-queue-floor-periods N`): after each successful
     write, if the queue is below the floor, insert a period of silence. The reasoning
     is what an underrun actually costs -- the driver stops the stream, and recovery
     `PREPARE`s it, which discards the WHOLE queued ring and slips the timeline by an
     arbitrary amount. A period of inserted silence costs 1.33 ms and slips nothing. So
     the choice is not "dropout or not" but "one bounded gap now or the entire queue
     later". Enforced after a write, so asking for N buys N-1 periods of worst-instant
     tolerance; the report prints both numbers so the off-by-one cannot be misread.

Why the band matters
--------------------
The natural level of the queue is what it is: for a 6-period ring at q=64 this host
holds a post-write median of about 3.9 periods, and it *climbs* there because a capture
burst is a second write and only a refusal sheds a period. Both controls are corrections
for excursions from that band, and the rule that came out of measuring them is symmetric:

    the CAP must sit ABOVE the band   -- otherwise it refuses ordinary bursts, and a
                                         refusal is a period of audio skipped.
    the FLOOR must sit BELOW the band  -- otherwise it fires on ordinary dips, and an
                                         insertion is 1.33 ms of audio delayed.

Both mistakes produce the same class of artefact, an audible hole, so the placement is
not a detail. The instrument in (1) is what makes the band visible at all.

The first run: floor INSIDE the band, and a limit cycle
------------------------------------------------------
`--periods 6 --output-queue-periods 5 --output-queue-floor-periods 4`, 900 s. Cap and
floor one period apart, with the queue's median sitting between them.

  driver xruns            0        (0 playback underruns, 0 capture overruns)
  queue                   min 0.86, last 3.88 periods; p1 3.73, p50 3.94
  blocks below 1 period   3 of 675 000   (0.0004 %)
  floor tolerance         3 periods = 4.000 ms at the worst instant
  floor insertions        71        (4544 frames = 94.667 ms of inserted silence)
  output drops            71        (all cap refusals)
  deadline overruns       55        chain p99 36.25 %, block p99 37.70 %
  RTF 0.9999, oracle exact over 240 000 frames

**71 insertions and 71 drops.** That is not a coincidence and it is the finding: at this
placement the cap and the floor are locked in a limit cycle. The queue touches the
ceiling, the cap refuses a period (a drop, minus one period), that leaves the next
post-write level under the floor, the controller inserts a period of silence (plus one
period), and the queue is straight back at the ceiling. One stall becomes a sustained
oscillation with an audible hole at each half-cycle.

So the controller did exactly what it was asked and made the audio *worse*: 142 audible
events in 15 minutes, against roughly 2 underruns that the un-floored configuration would
have produced in the same time at the same cap (the measured rate is 8 per hour). Zero
xruns is real, and it was bought at the wrong price.

The correction, and the next run
--------------------------------
Place both bounds outside the band: `--periods 6 --output-queue-periods 4
--output-queue-floor-periods 2` -- the cap above the measured p50 of 2.94 periods
pre-write, the floor well below it, one hour, against the hour at the same cap with no
floor (`soak-1h-ring6-cap4`: 8 playback underruns, 34 drops, 0 insertions). What that
run has to show is the floor earning its place: underruns down, insertions small, drops
unchanged.

And the conclusion that does not depend on the outcome
-----------------------------------------------------
Both controls work by creating a bounded hole -- a drop skips a period, an insertion
delays one -- so *any* configuration that corrects often is audible, whatever the bounds
are set to. A well-placed pair keeps the corrections rare, but "rare" over an hour is
still tens of events. The way out is not a better placement: it is making each hole
inaudible, which is `rt::SpliceFader` and the dropped-period edit kind in plan.md §10.3.
That work was scoped as a nicety while the queue was the story; measuring the floor is
what promoted it to the critical path.
