The output-queue floor: what it fixes, how to place it, and whether it earns its place
=====================================================================================

2026-09-15, `7a4d3dc`/`43dd110`, SSL 2+ Mk II (hw:2,0), 4 x A2-Full, q = 64 (1333.3 us), 48 kHz,
`SCHED_FIFO 80` + `--cpu 3`, non-RT kernel 7.0.0-1017-raspi. Evidence:
soak-15min-floor4.*, soak-1h-cap4-floor2.*, soak-ab-{a1,b1,b2,a2}.*, and the comparison hours
soak-1h-ring6-cap4.* in queue-target-sweep.txt.

What was added
--------------
Two things, because §9.5 item 8 had ended on a missing instrument and a mechanism it could not supply.

  1. The occupancy DISTRIBUTION. The harness keeps a histogram of the per-block queue occupancy, one
     bin per frame, clamped into the ring's length, and reports p1/p50 in periods plus the count of
     blocks whose queue held less than one period. That last number is the exposure window, and it is
     what `min` could never give: `playback_occupancy_min` is 0 in every run that underran once, so it
     describes the worst run rather than the typical one. 3 KB per run, exact percentiles, no sorting.

  2. The FLOOR CONTROLLER (`--output-queue-floor-periods N`): after each successful write, if the queue
     is below the floor, insert a period of silence. The reasoning is what an underrun actually costs --
     the driver stops the stream and recovery `PREPARE`s it, which discards the WHOLE queued ring and
     slips the timeline by an arbitrary amount -- against which a period of inserted silence (1.33 ms,
     no slip) is the cheaper mistake. Enforced after a write, so asking for N buys N-1 periods of
     worst-instant tolerance; the report prints both numbers so the off-by-one cannot be misread.

Why the band matters, and why the instrument had to come first
--------------------------------------------------------------
The queue's level is set by the ring, the bursts and the stalls: for a 6-period ring at q=64 this host
holds a post-write median near 3 periods, and it *climbs* there because a capture burst is a second
write and only a refusal sheds a period. Both controls correct excursions from that band, and both a
correction and its absence cost something:

    the CAP refuses ordinary bursts if it sits inside the band  -- a refusal skips a period of audio
    the FLOOR fires on ordinary dips if it sits inside the band  -- an insertion delays one

Neither bound is free, so the placement is not a detail, and the band is invisible without the
distribution instrument. That is why (1) came first and is not a nicety.

The first long run: both bounds inside the band, and a squeezed queue
--------------------------------------------------------------------
`--periods 6 --output-queue-periods 5 --output-queue-floor-periods 4`, 900 s. Floor and cap one period
apart, with the queue's median sitting between them:

  driver xruns            0
  queue                   p1 3.73, p50 3.94 periods; 3 of 675 000 blocks below one period
  floor tolerance         3 periods = 4.000 ms at the worst instant
  floor insertions        71   (94.667 ms of inserted silence)
  output drops            71   (all cap refusals)

**71 and 71** is a mechanical consequence of the placement rather than a coincidence. The controller
fires when the post-write queue is under 4 periods and the cap refuses when the pre-write queue is over
4, so the queue is *squeezed into a one-period band*: pre <= 256 frames and post >= 256 frames means
pre = 192 and post = 256, and every excursion across either edge is corrected. One correction per block
per edge is what "squeezed" looks like: 142 audible events in 15 minutes. Zero xruns, bought at a price
a listener would pay for.

Placement rule, derived rather than fitted:

    keep the bounds at least a couple of periods apart, and both OUTSIDE the band
    the queue actually occupies -- the cap above it, the floor below it.

The balanced A/B: does the floor earn its place?
------------------------------------------------
The hour at `--output-queue-periods 4 --output-queue-floor-periods 2` (both bounds outside the band)
reported 3 playback underruns against 8 for the same cap with no floor -- and also 65 insertions
against 0, 79 drops against 34, and a *timing* regression: chain p99 51.81 % against 36.39 %, block p99
53.96 % against 37.58 %, deadline overruns 874 against 204. The environment samples looked clean
(`loadavg` 0.29 then 0.24, `throttled=0x0`, 41-44 'C) and the overruns were spread across the whole
hour, but all phases were worse -- read, chain *and* write -- and the controller only touches the write
path. A single run cannot separate host drift from a knob.

So the comparison was re-run as a balanced design: the two configurations as **A B B A**, 900 s per
run, same cap of 4 periods, nothing else on the machine. Linear drift cancels between the arms.

  arm                     a1      b1      b2      a2     |  A mean   B mean
  floor periods            0       2       2       0     |     0        2
  ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
  queue p1 (periods)     2.70    2.77    2.77    2.78    |   2.74     2.77
  queue p50              3.06    2.94    3.03    3.11    |   3.09     2.99
  blocks < 1 period       30      11       9      19     |   24.5     10.0   (of 675 000)
  floor insertions         0      10      10       0     |    0       10
  output drops            18      14      18       8     |   13.0     16.0
  capture bursts          71      30      33      39     |   55       31.5
  playback underruns       4       1       2       2     |    3.0      1.5
  capture overruns         0       0       0       0     |    0        0
  chain p99 (% deadline) 38.65   34.29   33.74   33.19   |   35.92    34.02
  block p99 (% deadline) 40.05   35.60   35.02   34.54   |   37.30    35.31

Three readings:

  1. **The timing regression was the host, not the floor.** With drift cancelled, the floor arm is
     *faster*: chain p99 34.02 % against 35.92 %, block p99 35.31 % against 37.30 %. And the design
     measures its own noise: the same configuration swung 38.65 % -> 33.19 % between a1 and a2, a
     5.5-point spread, against a 1.9-point difference between the arms. Any single run of this
     comparison can manufacture or hide the effect completely, which is why it was run as A B B A.
  2. **The floor halves the exposure, which is the mechanism working.** Blocks holding less than one
     period: 30 and 19 without it, 11 and 9 with it -- 24.5 against 10.0 per 675 000 blocks, consistent
     in both directions and in both orderings. The queue's p1 barely moves (2.74 against 2.77) because
     the controller is holding the *bottom tail*, not the level: what it removes is exactly the blocks
     that were closest to starving.
  3. **The floor does not increase drops.** 13.0 against 16.0 per 900 s with ranges that overlap
     completely (8-18 both ways), so the "insertions are spent as cap drops" reading of the single hour
     was drift, not mechanism. Insertions cost about 10 per 900 s = 40 per hour = 53 ms of inserted
     silence per hour, against roughly 12 -> 6 playback underruns per hour.

Verdict, and the honest limits of it
------------------------------------
The floor controller does what it was built to do: it halves the exposure to starvation, roughly halves
a small underrun rate, does not increase drops, and does not cost latency. It is not free -- 40
inserted periods per hour, each one 1.33 ms of silence and each one audible without a splice fade.

The numbers are small enough that the *underrun* difference is suggestive rather than established (n=2
per arm, 3.0 against 1.5 events per 15 minutes), and the exposure difference is the stronger evidence
because it measures the mechanism rather than a rare event. Two things would settle it and neither is
done: a longer balanced design, and the occupancy at the instant a stall begins.

What to do with it:

  * **Ship it off by default** (it is), because the cost is audible today.
  * **With the splice fade of plan.md §10.3, enable it**: 40 inaudible 1.33 ms gaps per hour against
    ~6 underruns per hour, each of which discards the whole queued ring and slips the timeline. That is
    the trade the floor exists to offer, and the fade is what makes it a good one.
  * **Place the bounds as the rule says** -- at least a couple of periods apart and outside the band.
    Adjacent bounds squeeze the queue into a one-period band and correct every block.
  * The ring's size remains the only lever that reduces exposure without any hole at all; the bounds
    only decide which holes you get when it fails.
