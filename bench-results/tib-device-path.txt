The SSL 2+ does not work on tib's dwc2 USB controller — but the device path is fine on EHCI
=============================================================================================

2026-09-16. tib (RK3288, armv7, 7.2.6 PREEMPT_RT), performance governor capped 1416 MHz,
NEON armv7 build, all runs `--rt-priority 80 --cpu 3` with no isolation (tib has none).

THE FINDING
-----------
tib has two USB host controllers, and the four USB-A ports are not on the same one as the
other audio device:

  Bus 001  dwc2        root hub -> the on-board Genesys 4-port hub -> **the four USB-A ports**
  Bus 002  dwc2 (OTG)  gadget port, nothing attached
  Bus 003  ehci-platform  1 port -> the Realtek USB audio

`alsa_raw` cannot sustain playback on the SSL 2+ through **dwc2 + the on-board hub**, and it
is perfect through **EHCI**. Measured, passthrough only (no model, no DSP in the path):

  arm                        device / controller      quantum  xruns  RTF      queue p50
  tib-f0-pass   (60 s)       SSL / dwc2 + hub         64       739   1.0165   0.62 periods
  tib-f1-pass256 (60 s)      SSL / dwc2 + hub         256      180   1.0162   0.56
  tib-e1-ring12 (120 s)      3xA2-Lite, SSL, ring 12  64      1692   1.0190   0.62
  tib-e2-q128   (120 s)      3xA2-Lite, SSL, ring 6   128      684   1.0152   0.62
  tib-g0-realtek (60 s)      Realtek / **EHCI**       32         0   1.0000   0.25 (stable)

WHY IT IS NOT THE ENGINE
------------------------
In the passthrough arm the chain costs 2.3 us and the whole block is **24 us p50, 41 us p99** --
3% of the 1.333 ms period. There is no CPU pressure whatsoever (`cpu spike bursts: 1 block over
half a period` in the D2 arm) and the oracle is exact throughout.

The arithmetic identifies the mechanism exactly: **the run slips by precisely one period per
underrun.** 739 x 1.333 ms = 0.985 s over 60 s = the 1.65% RTF slip; 1692 x 1.333 ms = 2.256 s
over 120 s = the 1.90% slip in e1. So the underruns are not a symptom of falling behind; they
*are* the entire loss, and the loop is otherwise keeping up.

And the pacing number names the cause:

  SSL on dwc2:   device wait p50 1 471 463 ns   against a 1 333 333 ns period -- **the wait is
                 longer than the period**, so the loop can never pace itself off this device;
                 the ring drains to 0.00 (p50 0.62, and 35431 of 45000 blocks held less than one
                 period) and oscillates between empty and completely full (`536 blocks wrote
                 into a full playback ring`).
  Realtek on EHCI: device wait p50 3 974 844 ns against a 4 000 000 ns period -- 99.4% of the
                 period, and it holds **exactly** 0.25 periods of queue for the whole run with
                 RTF 1.0000.

A wait of 99.4% of the period is fine. A wait of 110% of it cannot be.

WHAT REMAINS OPEN, AND THE NEXT TEST
------------------------------------
Two candidates, and one swap separates them: the **SSL is a demanding device** (ASYNC playback
with an explicit sync endpoint and implicit feedback), or **dwc2's host-mode isochronous
scheduling** is the limitation (dwc2 is primarily an OTG/gadget controller). The on-board hub is
a third possibility but a weak one -- it is high-speed and passes 480 Mbit/s without a
transaction translator.

The test: move the SSL to the port the Realtek audio is on (bus 3, EHCI). If the SSL is clean
there, it is dwc2 and the fix is "use the EHCI port"; if it underruns there too, it is the
device's feedback mode and tib cannot host this interface at 48 kHz.

None of the ARMv7 conclusions depend on this: the throughput ceiling, the NEON finding, the
bit-identity verification and the deviceless loop pass are all deviceless measurements and are
unaffected. This finding is about tib's USB host controller, not about the engine.
