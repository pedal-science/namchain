Does cgroup-v2 cpuset confinement move the engine? Yes — measured on piv, 2026-09-17
=====================================================================================

RETRACTED (2026-09-17): the comparison below was not controlled
--------------------------------------------------------------
This file previously opened by claiming confinement took the engine from 15/4 xruns and 555/300
late blocks to 0 xruns and 19 late blocks, "same kernel, same device, same configuration, same
1800 s, the only difference being confinement". **That claim is withdrawn.** The two conditions
also differed in something much larger:

  * the isolation A/B's arms each ran about **a minute after a reboot**, and their overruns sit
    at the very start of the run -- first overrun at block **0**, 99, 20 and 239 of 1 350 000,
    with spike runs every 3-298 blocks through the first 0.4 s. That is the post-boot storm;
  * the confined arm was launched on a long-running system and had no storm at all: its first
    overrun is at block 44 544, 59 s in, and its spike bursts do not begin until block 35 911.

The start-of-arm load averages say the same thing: 6.63, 8.82, 8.45 and 7.25 for the four A/B
arms, against 0.40 for the confined arm. So that comparison measured the boot, not the
confinement, and the confinement may do nothing at all for the engine.

WHAT SURVIVES, AND WHAT IT IS WORTH
-----------------------------------
  * **The isolation A/B's own comparison survives**, because all four of its arms ran post-boot
    and in a balanced order (off/on/on/off), so the storm is common to both conditions and the
    difference between them is still attributable to the boot arguments: 79/115 xruns against
    15/4, and ~1010 against ~428 late blocks. Its *absolute* numbers are inflated by the storm,
    and its duration caveat (1800 s rather than the 3600 s requested) already stands.
  * **The vm-load pair is same-session and back to back**, and it says confinement's effect under
    load is small: 6 late blocks unconfined against 2 confined, p99 29.69% against 29.48%. Both
    arms are good, and both differ from the A/B arms mainly by not having been rebooted.
  * **A fix the A/B harness needs**: the driver must wait for the boot to settle before starting
    an arm. Two or three minutes of `sleep` at the top of `driver()` would have removed this
    entirely, and it is the same class of mistake as measuring on a machine that is still
    starting -- which the project has already been bitten by once.

The clean test is running as this file is written: `tools/soak/piv-confine-samesession-ab.sh`,
four arms in one session with **no reboots**, balanced unconfined/confined/confined/unconfined,
1800 s each. Confinement is a runtime property, so unlike the boot arguments it *can* be compared
without rebooting -- which is exactly why there is no excuse for the comparison above.
Evidence: `bench-results/piv-cx-*`.

WHY THIS NEEDED ITS OWN EXPERIMENT
----------------------------------
The first attempt (docs/distro-slimming.md section 7) confined the workload along with everything
else: `AllowedCPUs=` on the top-level slices applies to the measurement process too, so its
"confined" arms pushed the engine onto the loaded housekeeping cores and reported
"sched_setaffinity(cpu 3): Invalid argument". Those tables therefore compared two placements
rather than confinement, and its headline ("confinement takes driver xruns 746 -> 0") credited
confinement with improving a configuration it had not been applied to. Corrected in section 7.0
of that document; the method used here is:

  1. top-level slices held at 0-1 (all other work off the audio cores);
  2. the audio work in its own cpuset partition, `AllowedCPUs=2-3`, so it may still use them;
  3. rlimits passed to the unit -- a systemd service does not get pam_limits, and without
     `-p LimitRTPRIO=95` the identical invocation silently runs SCHED_OTHER. Verified: the smoke
     test reported `SCHED_FIFO priority 80, cpu 3` only after that property was added.

WHAT IS STILL RUNNING
---------------------
The matched vm-load pair, which answers the harder question their document leaves open -- whether
4 x A2-Full survives a *memory* load once the other work is genuinely confined:
  piv-cab-c-unconf-vm    unconfined, `stress-ng --vm 1 --vm-bytes 128M --vm-keep` on cpus 0-1
  piv-cab-d-confined-vm  confined,   the same load, engine in the audio partition
Driver: tools/soak/piv-confine-engine-ab.sh. Neither the confinement nor the load is persistent:
the slice properties are `--runtime` and are gone at reboot.


THE SAME-SESSION ANSWER (2026-09-17, 14:00-14:23)
-------------------------------------------------
Four arms in one session, **no reboots**, balanced unconfined / confined / confined / unconfined,
1800 s each, nothing else deliberate running (loadavg 0.39-0.65), engine pinned to cpu 3 in both
conditions and in its own cpuset partition for the confined ones. Every arm confirmed
`SCHED_FIFO priority 80, cpu 3`. Evidence: `bench-results/piv-cx-*`.

  arm             condition    xruns  late  blk p50  blk p99  blk max   p99%
  piv-cx-1-unconf unconfined       0     7      330      445     1931   33.38
  piv-cx-4-unconf unconfined       0     5      321      381     1376   28.60
  piv-cx-2-conf   confined         0     1      326      393     1339   29.49
  piv-cx-3-conf   confined         0     2      322      382     1428   28.68

  late blocks      unconfined 7, 5  ->  confined 1, 2       (means 6.0 -> 1.5, 4x fewer, and the
                                                            two conditions do not overlap)
  block p99 us     unconfined 445, 381 -> confined 393, 382 (means 413 -> 388, -6%)
  block max us     unconfined 1931, 1376 -> confined 1339, 1428 (means 1654 -> 1384, -16%)
  p99 % of deadline unconfined 33.38, 28.60 -> confined 29.49, 28.68 (means 31.0 -> 29.1)

**So confinement does move the engine, but modestly, and only the late-block count separates the
conditions cleanly at n=2.** The p99 and max differences are the same size as the spread *within*
the unconfined condition (cx-1 is markedly worse than cx-4 despite being the same configuration),
so they are a trend and not a result. There were **0 driver xruns and 0 queue drops in all four
arms**: on a quiet machine both conditions are good, and confinement buys a reduction in rare late
blocks rather than a change of regime.

That is a long way from the "15x" this file used to claim, and it is why the retraction above
matters: the large effect was the boot storm, and the modest one is what confinement is worth.


THE LOAD ITSELF IS NOT REPRODUCIBLE, WHICH IS THE BIGGER FINDING
----------------------------------------------------------------
Chasing a discrepancy turned up something more important than the confinement question.

`stress-ng --vm 1 --vm-bytes 128M` on cpus 0-1, 4 x A2-Full, q64, 300 s:

  arm                engine config                     load flags        xruns   late    p99 us   max us
  piv-d3-retest      cap 4, no splice                  --vm, no keep      5467   32875    2756    10527
  rt-piv-d3-vm       cap 4, no splice                  --vm, no keep      5460   38210    2793    10020
  piv-vm-nokeep      cap 3 + splice                    --vm, no keep      2400   26719    2050    10441
  piv-vm-keep        cap 3 + splice                    --vm-keep           562   10083    1597     6148
  piv-cab-c-unconf-vm cap 3 + splice                   --vm-keep             0       6     396     1431

Two readings.

1. **`--vm-keep` matters, and it is worth about 4x on xruns in a controlled pair** (2400 -> 562,
   both arms back to back this afternoon, same engine configuration). Without it stress-ng frees
   and re-maps its 128 MB every cycle, so the kernel zeroes and re-faults those pages continuously;
   with it, the same pages are rewritten in place. So the thing that hurts the engine is **new-page
   traffic -- faults, zeroing, mapping churn -- not the size of the working set.** That is a real
   refinement of docs/tuning.md, whose rule currently says "do not run memory-heavy work beside the
   engine" without saying what makes it heavy.
2. **The same flags do not reproduce across sessions.** `piv-cab-c-unconf-vm` and `piv-vm-keep`
   are the same configuration and the same load flags, 3.5 hours apart: **0 driver xruns against
   562**, and 6 late blocks against 10 083. Meanwhile `piv-d3-retest` reproduced `rt-piv-d3-vm`
   to within 7 xruns a day apart (5467 against 5460), so it is not that the machine cannot repeat
   itself -- it is that *this* comparison sits near a cliff. A small uncontrolled difference flips
   the engine between "fine" and "thousands of xruns", which is exactly the regime where a
   single-arm measurement means nothing and only a balanced same-session design does.

**Consequence for how this project measures from here on:** on piv, any arm that involves memory
pressure must be run in a balanced same-session sequence, and the manipulation check must record
the *load's* own traffic (page faults or equivalent), not just its presence. The confinement
question could be answered cleanly because confinement is a runtime property; the memory-pressure
question has no such luxury and needs the pairing.
