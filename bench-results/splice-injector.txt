The splice injector: it found a real bug immediately, and the aggregate question is still open
=============================================================================================

2026-09-15 late evening, working tree on top of `5b011e7`. Evidence: soak-60s-injected.*,
soak-8min-paired.*, soak-20min-paired.*, and the earlier soak-5min-bridge.*.

Why the injector exists
-----------------------
The repair can only be judged on an *isolated* splice with clean audio either side, and a real session
cannot be asked to produce one: the same cap-4 configuration gave 13-18 drops per 900 s one afternoon
and **zero in 50 minutes** the next evening. Waiting for the bad afternoon is not an experiment.
`--inject-splice-every S` loses one output period on a schedule instead, exactly as a refusal loses it
(the ring drains, the engine still renders), so the geometry the bridge sees is the real one and the
injected count is reported separately from real drops. It is the device-path version of §10.3's "500
randomised edits" torture render.

What it found, first run: the bridge was anchored on periods the device never heard
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The debug trace over one injected splice, before the fix:

    device's last emitted sample (raw[255])          -0.0513
    the bridge's first emitted sample (emitted[256])  0.1331     <- should have been -0.0211
    raw step it was supposed to remove                0.162

`rt::SpliceFader::apply()` advanced the anchor -- the sample the next repair leaves from -- for every
period it *computed*. But a period that is then refused or injected is never written, so the device
never hears it, and the next bridge was leaving from the last thing the loop *computed* rather than the
last thing it *played*. With 12 refusals and 29 injections in a minute, that is most of the repairs
anchored in the wrong place.

The unit tests could not see this: they hand the fader every period, so computing and writing are the
same event there. **This is the strongest argument yet for the on-device verifier existing** -- the
detector chose the repair's shape, and the device found the loop's mistake.

Fixed by splitting the two events: `apply()` computes and `commit()` records, and the loop commits only
when the write was taken. Both are now pinned by tests, including one that emits without committing and
asserts the anchor has *not* moved.

Resolved: the span was skipping the period the device last heard
---------------------------------------------------------------
The regression trace answered it, and the fault was in the verifier's geometry, not in the bridge.
`join` was placed *at* the history's last period instead of after it, so the span was four periods of
history followed by the period after the splice -- and the period the device had actually heard last,
sitting at the history's tail, was overwritten by the new one and left out. The bridge, which correctly
continues that period, was therefore being measured against the period *before* it, and it looked worse
than the raw splice because it was bridging to the wrong neighbour. The trace said so directly: the
bridge's first emitted sample was `history_last + slope` to five decimals of the anchor it was supposed
to have, while the samples either side showed a step against a period it did not continue.

With the span corrected -- the whole history, then the period after the splice -- 29 injected splices in
60 s, one every 2 s, with 12 real refusals alongside:

    raw splice   0.301792   = 4.3x the signal's own reference
    bridged      0.064053   = 1.0x the signal's own reference

The repaired splice's worst second difference now equals the audio's own worst curvature elsewhere in the
same span: by the detector's measure the splice is no longer distinguishable from the signal. That is the
claim the bridge was built for, measured on the device's own audio rather than on a test tone, with the
oracle still exact (`max|diff| = 0` over 240 000 frames) and 0 render-path allocations in the same run.

What the four bugs cost and bought
----------------------------------
All four were found by the verifier, and none was visible from the unit tests:

  1. the detector's guard is a model transition's 5 ms, wider than the span, so it rejected the span
     outright -- and reporting nothing looked exactly like finding nothing wrong;
  2. the spacing guard deferred the recording instead of aborting it, judging a block where no bridge
     was acting;
  3. the span omitted the last period the device heard, which inverted the measurement;
  4. `apply()` advanced the anchor for periods the loop then refused, so the bridge left from the last
     thing it computed rather than the last thing it played. The unit tests hand the fader every period,
     where computing and writing are the same event, so this one was invisible by construction.

Every one was found by comparing the emitted signal against itself on the device. That is the argument
for the verifier existing rather than trusting the synthetic case -- the detector chose the repair's
*shape*, and the device found where the *loop* got it wrong.


The insertion path, measured on device: not yet continuous
----------------------------------------------------------
`--inject-insert-every S` (the insertion half of the injector) plus the verifier extended to insertions
-- the window covering entry, payload and exit, so a payload cannot pass by having its entry left out.
The number below is not comparable with the drop numbers: it judges a whole inserted period at once,
where a drop is judged over eight samples after the join.

    --periods 12 --splice-fade-ms 1.333 --inject-insert-every 10, 90 s
    8 insertions inserted deliberately, 8 judged, 0 output drops, 0 driver xruns
    worst second difference 0.272028 against a reference of 0.070685  =  3.8x

So the device's insertion path is **not** continuous, while the unit test says the shaped payload plus the
loop's exit bridge passes every rule. That gap is the finding, and the run is clean enough to believe it:
no cap drops, no underruns, injections ten seconds apart so every span had five splice-free periods of
history -- the guard that *had* to be added first, because with 26 cap drops alongside, the same
measurement read 5.0x and was describing the neighbouring splices rather than the payload.

Two things to read first, and the reason the drop case took four bugs to close:

  * the verifier *reconstructs* the payload from `fader.last_emitted(0)` and `fader.last_slope(0)`, while
    the backend built the one it wrote from the two frames of *device-format* audio it had kept. Those
    should agree to within quantisation, and each is separately plausible -- which is exactly the shape of
    the bug that inverted the drop measurement, where the reconstructed span differed from the real one.
  * the injected insertion adds a period of queue, so an injection is only isolated while there is
    headroom: at ring 6 with one every 2 s it produced 27 refusals of its own, and the conservation law
    is unforgiving -- every inserted period is eventually paid for by a refusal or an underrun.

The next step is the diagnostic that closed the drop case, unchanged: dump the samples either side of the
entry, through the payload, to the exit, and compare the verifier's reconstruction against what the
backend actually wrote. Nothing else should change until that is read.
