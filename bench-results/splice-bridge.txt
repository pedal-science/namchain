The splice bridge: how the detector picked the shape, and what is left
======================================================================

2026-09-15, `rt::SpliceFader` + `--splice-fade-ms`, on top of `7a4d3dc`/`43dd110`. Evidence:
tests/test_splice_fader.cpp (the derivation, as tests), soak-5min-bridge.* (the wiring on hardware),
and the queue work it follows in queue-floor.txt / queue-target-sweep.txt.

What the artefact is
--------------------
Two things put a step in the emitted waveform, and both are period-sized holes:

  * a DROPPED period -- the queue cap or the ring refused a write. The ring is a FIFO of period-sized
    buffers, so skipping an append means the device plays out(k-1) and then out(k+1): a 64-frame jump,
    1.33 ms of missing signal, a step big enough to hear as a click above a few hundred hertz.
  * an INSERTED period -- the floor controller keeping the queue off the bottom. The step is at its
    entry, where a payload arrives mid-signal without continuing it.

Neither can be repaired where it happens: by the time the decision is made the previous period is
already in the ring, and queued audio cannot be rewritten. So the repair happens on the way out, on the
periods that follow, and it is a **loop policy rather than a model property** -- it acts on the emitted
copy of the signal, which is what lets the engine's output stay untouched and the oracle stay exact.

The detector picked the shape, and rejected three others
-------------------------------------------------------
plan.md §10.3 requires the glitch detector to exist before the transition machinery it judges, and this
is that order being used: `tests/test_splice_fader.cpp` builds the emitted stream twice -- spliced raw,
and spliced then repaired -- and runs `glitch::analyse` over the join. The oracle for a repair is the
*raw* spliced signal, since neither is more faithful than the other (a drop loses 64 frames and no fade
brings them back); what the repair buys is continuity, so continuity is what is measured.

Three shapes looked reasonable and all three were rejected by the rule, with numbers:

  * **hold the last sample.** The first difference at the join is zero and the continuity rule measures
    a *second* difference, so a hold scores |d| -- the signal's own first difference. On a 997 Hz tone at
    0.4 that is 0.052 against a 0.0136 limit: 3.8x over, and 7.7x the reference it is compared with.
  * **decaying extrapolation**, `a1 + d*(1-w)`. Zeroes the second difference at the join and puts it all
    back at the next sample, because the slope collapses from d to zero in one step: measured 0.0521,
    no better than the hold.
  * **a 5 ms crossfade.** Holds the value for its first samples and attenuates the signal for 5 ms, which
    is a swell, not a repair -- and it still fails, for the hold's reason.

Matching **value and slope** is what makes both differences small. The bridge is a cubic Hermite:

    d  = a1 - a0                    the slope the device was last travelling at
    xs = x1 - x0                    the slope the new signal starts with
    e0 = (a1 + d) - x0              value mismatch, against the *continuation* rather than a hold
    m0 = (d - xs) * (K-1)           slope mismatch
    y[i] = x[i] + e0*h00(t) + m0*h10(t),   t = i/(K-1)

with h00, h10 the Hermite bases. The value at the join is `a1 + d`, not `a1`: that single sample of
extrapolation is what makes the second difference *exactly zero* there, which is the best score the rule
admits. What is left is the two mismatches, taken smoothly to zero over K frames, so the residual is
~|e0|/K^2 + |m0|/K.

The window length, measured rather than chosen
----------------------------------------------
The one free parameter is K, and the detector bounds it. Worst second difference inside the bridge, on
the same 997 Hz tone, against a reference of 0.0068 and a limit (kappa = 2) of 0.0136:

    K =   4    16     32     64      128     240
        0.0477 0.0213 0.0142 0.0097  0.0082  0.0079

Longer is monotonically better for continuity, and worse for the signal: the offset that does the
bridging is held for the whole window, so a long window is a long swell. K = 64 -- **one period, the
quantum the whole system is already built around** -- is the shortest window that passes, with about
30 % margin, and it is the default the tests pin. The window-length test exists because the first
version of the code recomputed the mismatches on *every* period of the bridge instead of freezing them
at its start, which re-injected the mismatch each period and left the offset never reaching zero. That
bug was invisible at K = 64 (a single period) and showed up only at K = 240, which is why the test
compares a short window with a long one.

What is left: the entry of an inserted period
---------------------------------------------
The bridge repairs the *exit* of an insertion and cannot reach its entry, because the loop never writes
that period -- the backend does. The backend's payload is now the last frame written, repeated, rather
than silence, which turns a step in *value* into a break in *slope*: measured on a 313 Hz tone, the
entry step falls from 0.4 (silence) to under 0.05 (a hold), and the worst second difference in the
window falls by more than 4x. The rule is still tripped, and the test asserts that rather than pretending
otherwise. Closing it means the backend writing a *bridged* payload using the same two Hermite terms --
a backend change, not a loop one, and the named remainder of this repair.

On the device
-------------
`--periods 6 --output-queue-periods 3 --output-queue-floor-periods 2 --splice-fade-ms 1.333`, 300 s,
4 x A2-Full, pinned and self-scheduled (`soak-5min-bridge.*`):

    splice repair    64-frame bridge (1.333 ms) armed 6 times: 3 dropped periods, 3 inserted ones
    driver xruns     0   (0 playback underruns, 0 capture overruns)
    output drops     3      floor insertions  3      capture bursts  1
    queue            p1 1.59, p50 1.94 periods; 3 of 225 000 blocks below one period
    render path      0 allocations, 0 frees, 0 blocks descheduled
    oracle           match, max|diff| = 0 over 240 000 frames

Both splice causes are exercised, and the oracle is exact in the same run: the bridge repaired the
emitted signal and the engine's own output was untouched. Six bridges in five minutes is what the
configuration produces; the block p99 in this run (49.9 %) is higher than the A/B's 34-37 % and is host
drift, not the bridge -- six bridges of 64 samples cannot register against a 1333 us deadline, and the
A/B in queue-floor.txt measured exactly that kind of drift at 5.5 points on the same configuration.

Two claims that stay independent
--------------------------------
The bridge writes `mono_emit`; the oracle keeps comparing `mono_out`. So "the engine is sample-exact
against the offline render" and "the emitted signal is continuous" are separate measurements of the same
run rather than one measurement used twice -- which is the only reason the faded run can still be quoted
as evidence about the engine at all.
