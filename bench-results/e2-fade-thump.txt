The fade adds up to 3 dB when the two signals are correlated: a thump at every handover
=========================================================================================

2026-10-07. Found by listening, not by the detector: the owner heard "a quiet, short 'thud' at each
point where there's a switch from full to lite", clearest in the quiet last seconds of the
Ampeg SVT - MD 421 set. Tool: namchain 083de32 plus the `--fade-shape` and `fade excess` additions
below; the same four-model chains and 52-model sweep as e2-lite-vs-full.txt, on the same input.

The cause
---------

`rt::Crossfade` (include/namchain/rt/crossfade.h) is deliberately equal-power: weights
g1 = cos(t*pi/2), g2 = sin(t*pi/2), so that g1^2 + g2^2 == 1 across the fade, on the argument that a
linear fade has w^2 + (1-w)^2 = 0.5 at the midpoint, "a 3 dB dip in the middle of every transition,
which is audible on sustained material". That argument is right for signals that are **uncorrelated**
and wrong for signals that are **correlated**: the power of g1*a + g2*b is

    g1^2 + g2^2 + 2*rho*g1*g2

which equal power takes to be 1. That is true only at rho = 0. At the midpoint with rho = 1 it is
2, i.e. **+3 dB**, and it is a *bump* rather than a dip: the fade adds energy where the two signals
are alike. Lite and Full are two models of the same rig, so rho is 0.91 to 1.00, and the bump lands
in the low end because that is where both models put their energy -- a thud.

Two different library models, by contrast, are barely correlated at all (measured over the whole
input: -0.72, -0.60, -0.45, +0.29, +0.47), so a *rig-to-rig* fade is in the regime equal power was
chosen for and is not the problem. The problem is every fade where the two sides carry nearly the
same signal: a Lite/Full handover, a bypass toggle's wet/dry, a model swap within a slot.

Why the glitch detector never saw it
------------------------------------

None of its four rules can. The bump is continuous (rule 1 looks for a step), it is the opposite of
a dropout (rule 2 looks for digital black), it adds no energy above 5 kHz in excess (rule 3), and
the fade does converge to the new signal (rule 4). A level bump is invisible to all four, which is
why 102 of 104 model-directions were reported "clean" while every one of them was thumping.

The bench now measures it: `fade excess`, the worst peak inside the fade against the louder of the
two signals, in dB, with the correlation it measured for that switch.

The measurement (52 models, 11 switch points each, both directions: 104 rows)

    shape                     rows with a finding    fade excess: worst   median    best
    equal-power (today)                        2            +3.01 dB   +2.88   +2.60
    equal-gain                                 0            -0.00 dB   +0.00   -0.09
    compensated                                0            +0.03 dB   -0.00   -0.10

**Every one of the 104 rows bumps by 2.6 to 3.0 dB with the current law.** Both replacements remove
it, and both also clear the two marginal findings the equal-power sweep had (continuity at 55.00 s
on two SVT models). On the model the owner flagged, Ampeg SVT - MD 421 at a switch every second:

    shape          Lite->Full                    Full->Lite                    excess
    equal-power    47/56 clean, d2 x4.2         47/56 clean, d2 x3.9          +2.90 / +2.88 dB
    equal-gain     56/56 clean, d2 x0.9         56/56 clean, d2 x0.9          +0.00 / +0.00 dB
    compensated    56/56 clean, d2 x1.2         56/56 clean, d2 x0.9          +0.00 / +0.00 dB

The fixes
---------

**equal-gain** is (1-k, k): exact for rho = 1, 3 dB down at the midpoint for rho = 0. Right for
this handover, and wrong in the case the current comment worries about.

**compensated** divides both weights by sqrt(1 + 2*rho*g1*g2): exact at both ends of the range and
the same shape in between. It reduces to equal power for uncorrelated material and to constant
amplitude for identical material. **This is the one to use generally**, and it costs nothing on the
render path: `rt::Crossfade` already builds its weight tables in `start()`, so the compensation is
one extra sqrt per table entry when the fade starts, not per sample.

**rho has to be clamped to [0, 1], and this matters.** Written without the clamp the compensation
made the thump *worse* (+4.6 dB on MD 421): a 20 ms estimate of rho over near-silence comes out
negative, and a negative rho divides by less than one, amplifying the fade. The bench clamps, and
documents why.

What this means for the engine
------------------------------

The engine's fade is the same law: `rt::Crossfade`, used by `nc.xfade` for local transitions
(bypass flips, model swaps) and by the whole-chain fade for rig switches. So a bypass toggle, where
the two signals are the same audio, is the rho ~ 1 case and has this thump today; a rig switch
between two different amps is the rho ~ 0 case and does not. The change is small -- a correlation
per fade, and the table built from it -- but it is every transition in the engine, so it wants its
own commit and its own transition tests rather than being slipped into E2.

What this does not establish
- Whether equal-gain's 3 dB *dip* on genuinely uncorrelated material is audible: it is the failure
  mode the current comment is protecting against, and the compensated law avoids both. Not measured
  here because the compensating law makes the question moot.
- The right way to estimate rho in the engine: a short lookback at fade start is what the bench
  does (20 ms). A bypass fade could simply declare rho = 1, since it knows the two signals are the
  same audio.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2026-10-07, the engine side: measured through the engine's own code, then fixed
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The measurement the plan asked for, in `tests/test_crossfade.cpp`, through the three paths the
engine's fade is reached by. `fade excess` is the worst peak inside the fade against the louder of
the two signals it is mixing, in dB:

  path                                            before     after
  rt::Crossfade, the same audio on both sides    +2.98 dB    +0.00
  a plan-level transition (a bypass flip)        +3.01 dB    +0.00
  the nc.xfade node a local transition is built from
                                                 +3.01 dB    +0.00
  rt::Crossfade, two unrelated signals           -0.04 dB    -0.04   (the case equal power is for)

All three are red before the fix and green after, and the fourth is the control that says the law is
still equal power where it should be. The engine's existing transition test could not have caught
this: its fixture crossfades two plans that differ by a gain, so the two signals are *perfectly*
correlated, and it passed every glitch rule while thumping -- because a level bump is continuous, is
not a dropout, adds no excess high frequency and converges.

A second defect fell out of the first. `RenderPlan::prewarm` runs one silent block through the whole
plan, and `nc.xfade` -- which is `skip_prewarm` precisely so that "the fade is not spent on silence
before the plan is heard" -- was run anyway: `skip_prewarm` was only honoured by `prewarm_fresh`.
Every local transition therefore entered its first audible block 64 frames into a 240-frame fade:
3.7 ms of crossfade where 5 ms was asked for. It also meant the fade's correlation measurement
compared silence with silence. `prewarm` now skips those nodes too, and a test asserts where the
fade's midpoint lands (a constant input with its inverse on the other side is g1 - g2, zero at the
midpoint, so the midpoint's position says where the fade started).

The fix
-------

`rt::Crossfade` keeps the one equal-power table it had and normalises the weights as it reads them,
by `1/sqrt(1 + 2*rho*g1*g2)`: exact at every rho rather than at a few, one sqrt and one divide per
sample for the length of a fade, and nothing at all when rho measures 0. (Five precomputed tables
were the first cut and cost 655 kB per daemon chain -- 8 chains at kMaxTransitionFrames -- for less
accuracy than this.) `start(frames, rho)` takes a rho, and the default measures it from the first
block it is handed, before writing any of it, so no sample is mixed with the wrong law. Measuring rather than declaring is what the call sites need: what a
transition joins is not known when it starts -- a bypass of a NAM model is two unrelated signals, a
bypass of a gain is the same audio twice, and an EQ ramp is the same audio through two filters -- and
the block is in hand before the output is written. rho is clamped to [0, 1], because a negative
estimate (a 1.33 ms window of near-silence produces one readily) would divide by less than one and
amplify the fade: written without the clamp in the bench, it turned a +2.9 dB thump into +4.6 dB.

Nothing else changed: the render path still allocates nothing (the existing allocation test covers
it), `start` stays O(1) on the render path, and the daemon and the three node types that fade --
`nc.xfade`, `nc.eq.biquad`'s two lanes, `nc.delay`'s two taps -- all pass both signals to `mix`
before it writes, so all four measure rather than guess. The whole suite passes, on the Mac and in
the aarch64 container build: 341 cases, 270,069 assertions.
